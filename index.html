Web3

The vault that can’t see the world

Web3 is an internet where the rules live on a shared network anyone can check, with no company in the middle. It’s powerful, but it has one big blind spot. Scroll to find out what it is and how Pyth fixes it.

Why “Web3”? Gavin Wood, a co-founder of Ethereum, coined it around 2014. Web1 was read-only, Web2 was read-and-write, and Web3 adds own.

Chapter 1

The smart contract

Think of a vending machine. Put in the right coin, press the right button, and the snack drops. No cashier decides anything. A smart contract is that machine for money. You can program it to say “If Ethereum hits $4,000, unlock the funds.”

Computer scientist Nick Szabo coined “smart contract” in the 1990s. His own example was a vending machine, which is why that analogy has stuck.

The problem

The blind spot

Picture a judge locked in a windowless room. The judge follows the rules perfectly but can’t see outside. Our vault is the same: it lives inside the blockchain and has no idea what Ethereum costs in the real world. It needs a trusted messenger.

That messenger is called an oracle, from the Latin orare, “to speak.” Ancient Greeks traveled to Delphi to hear a priestess speak for the god Apollo.

Enter Pyth Network

Pyth is the messenger. It pulls real-time prices straight from the exchanges and trading firms that make the market, then streams them into the vault.

The priestess at Delphi was called the Pythia. Pyth is named after her: an oracle that speaks the truth, this time about prices.

The old way

The single price myth

Many oracles hand a contract one exact price. But no asset trades at the same price on every exchange at the same millisecond. Look at the orbiting dots: each is an exchange.

Market chaos

When volatility spikes

In a crash the exchanges drift far apart. If an oracle forces one price on-chain anyway, protocols get exploited and people are liquidated at prices that were never fair.

The fix

The confidence interval

Pyth publishes the price and how unsure it is right now. You’ve seen this on a phone map: the blue dot sits inside a circle that grows when GPS is weak. The purple ring around the sphere is that circle.

Safe range: waiting for data…

“Confident” comes from Latin confidere, “to trust fully.” “Interval” comes from inter vallum, the space between two ramparts: a range with walls on both sides.

Putting it together

  • Smart contract: a vending machine for money
  • Oracle: the messenger that tells it what’s true outside
  • Pyth: a messenger that also admits how sure it is

Contracts that know the uncertainty can pause, widen safety margins or refuse a risky trade, instead of acting on a price that was never exact.
